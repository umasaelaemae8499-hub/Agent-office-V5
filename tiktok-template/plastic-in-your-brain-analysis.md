## 3. The formula, and why it works

1. **Hook = headline screenshot + "Scientists just found out…"** (0–2 s). Borrowed authority from a real news outlet, plus a "you're affected" claim ("*our* brains").
2. **Stat in the first 8 seconds** ("6% per year"). A concrete number makes the claim believable.
3. **Method → result → comparison** (9–42 s). He explains *how* they knew, then a big number (+50%, 7g), then a **comparison to an everyday object** (a plastic spoon, a human hair). The comparison is the most shareable line.
4. **Escalation** (48–58 s): "To make matters worse…" adds a second, scarier finding (10× in dementia).
5. **Face appears late.** The first 55 s are pure visuals, so viewers stay for the "show". The face comes in for opinion and humour, which builds a personal brand.
6. **Two humour beats** (the Breaking Bad cut at 12 s, the Action Man joke at 70 s) relieve the doom and get rewatches and shares.
7. **Pun CTA**: "maintaining the plasticity of your brain. Follow for more." It ties back to the topic.
8. **Consistent visual system**: a dark navy grid "lab", a pink 3D brain, cyan particles, big white sans-serif numbers, teal label boxes, red arrows. Every graphic looks like part of one set.
9. **Captions**: white, ALL CAPS, heavy sans (Montserrat ExtraBold style), thin dark outline, 2–4 words per caption, lower-middle of the frame.

>
> **⚠️ Fact-check update (2026):** the original video is based on *Nihart et al., "Bioaccumulation of
> microplastics in decedent human brains", Nature Medicine, Feb 2025* (University of New Mexico).
> Since then:
> - Germany's BfR (Federal Institute for Risk Assessment) and a Nov 2025 "matters arising" reply in the journal said the method (Py-GC-MS) can mistake **brain fat for polyethylene**, so the amounts are likely **overestimated**.
> - A later study (Nature Health) of **191 brain samples** found about **50 µg/g**, roughly **100× lower**, but still found plastic in **nearly all** samples.
>
> So "a spoonful of plastic in your brain" is **disputed**. "There is plastic in our brains, and how much is hotly debated" is **solid**.
> Ben's video is now outdated, which is your angle: **"The viral 'plastic spoon in your brain' claim: what's actually true."**
> An update or debunk of a viral video gets the hook *and* the credibility.
